# The Patrón — Charro beans

Pinto beans simmered with pork, bacon, poblano, tomato and beer.

- **Makes:** About 5 cups
- **Source:** Rick Bayless, Authentic Mexican (frijoles charros)
- **First step:** 7:00 AM · Your kitchen · morning
- **Last step:** 7:26 PM · Her kitchen

## Equipment

- Big heavy pot with a lid (4 quarts or larger)
- Skillet
- Tongs
- Bowl and a plate to cover it
- Strainer
- Slotted spoon
- Kitchen scissors
- Heatproof mug
- Bean tub with a lid (2 quarts)
- Wooden spoon
- Can opener
- Oven mitt or a thick dry towel

## Ingredients

- **½ pound** dried pinto beans, about 1¼ cups, half the bag
- **4 cups** water, for soaking
- **3½ cups** fresh water, for cooking
- **½ cup** beer
- **2 ounces** pork, 3 or 4 chunks of the stew meat, cut in ½-inch pieces
- **4 slices** thick-cut bacon, in ½-inch pieces
- **½ medium** white onion, one wedge of your jumbo onion, diced
- **1 large** poblano chile, your store calls it pasilla; blackened, peeled, seeded, chopped
- **Half a can** plain diced tomatoes, drained
- **½ teaspoon** salt, or to taste
- **¼ cup** cilantro, roughly chopped, stirred in at dinner

## Steps

Some steps say “tap” or mention the guide. That is the phone app. On paper, set your own timer for every **Timer** line.

### Your kitchen · morning

1. **7:00 AM — Did the beans soak overnight?** (**30 sec**)
    - If you covered them with water last night, tap Yes.
    - If not, tap No. There is a **1-hour** shortcut. The beans may have to be finished at her place.
    - Yes, they soaked. These steps are written for this answer. Go on.
    - No, they did not. See “If you did not soak the beans” below.
2. **7:00 AM — Drain the beans** (**2 min**)
    - Pour the beans into the strainer in the sink.
    - Rinse them under cold water for **a few seconds**.
    - You need:
        - Soaked beans
        - Strainer
3. **7:08 AM — Cut the pork small** (**3 min**)
    - You need 2 ounces of pork. That is 3 or 4 chunks of the stew meat, a small handful.
    - Snip each chunk into ½-inch pieces with kitchen scissors, into a small bowl. ½ inch is about the width of your little finger.
    - Doing the quick soak? Cover the bowl and put it in the fridge. The pork goes in the pot in about **an hour**.
    - No scissors? Cut it on a dinner plate, not on your cutting board. Wash the plate with hot soapy water after.
    - Wrap the rest of the pork and freeze it.
    - You need:
        - Stew pork
        - Kitchen scissors
        - Small bowl
    - **Warning:** Raw pork. Wash your hands and the scissors with hot soapy water.
4. **7:11 AM — Fill the bean pot** (**3 min**)
    - Into the big pot: the drained beans, 3½ cups of water, ½ cup of beer, and the pork.
    - Put the rest of the beer in the fridge. Wash the empty pork bowl with hot soapy water.
    - You need:
        - Drained beans
        - Measuring cup
        - Beer
        - Pork (in the fridge if you cut it earlier)
    - Goes into: Big pot
5. **7:14 AM — Beans on medium heat** (**1 min**)
    - Burner on medium. No lid yet.
    - Tap Done now. A **12-minute** timer starts.
    - Go on to the next step while it heats. The guide calls you back.
    - **Timer: 12 minutes.** Beans coming to a boil. Keep working. It ends about 7:27 AM.
    - When it ends: Turn the beans down.
6. **7:28 AM — Turn the beans down** (**1 min**)
    - Look in the bean pot. When you see steady bubbles, turn the burner down to medium-low.
    - You want a simmer: a few small bubbles, not a hard boil.
    - Gray foam on top is normal. Skim it off with a spoon or leave it.
    - Put the lid on, slightly open.
    - Stir them now and then. The guide will remind you. If beans poke above the liquid, add a splash of water.
    - **Timer: 1 hour.** Beans simmering. Keep working. It ends about 8:29 AM.
    - At 7:49 AM and 8:09 AM: Stir the beans. Add water if they poke out.
    - When it ends: Are the beans tender?
    - Look for: A few lazy bubbles every second.
    - Tip: Not bubbling yet? Turn the heat up a notch and give it **3 more minutes**.
7. **7:33 AM — Blacken the poblano** (**8 min**)
    - You are burning the skin on purpose.
    - Gas stove: turn a burner to high. Lay the chile on the metal grate, right in the flame.
    - Turn it with tongs every **30 seconds** until the skin is black and blistered all over, **5 to 8 minutes**.
    - No gas flame: move an oven rack to about 4 inches below the top element. Turn the broiler to high.
    - Put the chile on a foil-lined pan on that rack. Turn it with tongs every **2 to 3 minutes**, about **10 minutes**. Use an oven mitt.
    - Drop the hot chile in a bowl and cover the bowl with a plate. Burner or broiler off.
    - You need:
        - Poblano (labeled pasilla)
        - Tongs
        - Bowl and plate
        - No gas flame: foil-lined pan and oven mitt
    - **Timer: 5 minutes.** Poblano steaming. Keep working. It ends about 7:46 AM.
    - When it ends: Peel and chop the poblano.
    - Look for: Black on every side. No big green patches.
    - Tip: The skin may pop or catch small flames. That is normal. Keep turning.
    - **Warning:** Open flame. Keep sleeves, towels and paper away from it. Turn on the fan over the stove.
8. **7:41 AM — Cut the bacon** (**3 min**)
    - Take 4 slices of bacon.
    - Snip them with kitchen scissors into ½-inch pieces, straight into the cold skillet.
    - No scissors? Stack the slices on a dinner plate and cut them with a knife.
    - Wrap the rest of the bacon. Fridge or freezer.
    - You need:
        - Bacon
        - Kitchen scissors
        - Skillet
    - **Warning:** Raw pork. Wash your hands and the scissors with hot soapy water.
9. **7:44 AM — Start the bacon** (**1 min**)
    - Burner on medium-low under the skillet.
    - Tap Done now. A **10-minute** timer starts.
    - Stir the bacon now and then while you do the next steps. The guide will remind you.
    - **Timer: 10 minutes.** Bacon frying. Keep working. It ends about 7:55 AM.
    - At 7:49 AM and 7:52 AM: Stir the bacon.
    - When it ends: Take the bacon out.
10. **7:45 AM — Dice a wedge of white onion** (**5 min**)
    - Your white onions are jumbo, so a quarter of one equals the recipe's half onion.
    - Cut a thin slice off both ends of one onion. Stand it on a cut end. Cut it in half from top to bottom.
    - Lay each half flat side down and cut it in half again. Peel the 4 wedges.
    - Dice one wedge into small pieces, about ¼ inch.
    - Wrap the other three wedges and put them in the fridge. One is for the rice purée. One goes to her place for the ceviche. One is a spare.
    - You need:
        - 1 white onion
        - Cutting board
        - Knife
    - Goes into: Small bowl. The chopped poblano joins it later.
11. **7:50 AM — Drain half the can of tomatoes** (**2 min**)
    - Open the plain diced tomatoes. Not the fire-roasted can.
    - Pour about half the can into the strainer over the sink. Let it drip for **a minute**. Then set the strainer on a plate next to the stove.
    - They are already cut small, so no chopping.
    - You do not need the other half. Fridge it or toss it.
    - You need:
        - Plain diced tomatoes
        - Can opener
        - Strainer
        - Plate
12. **7:52 AM — Peel and chop the poblano** (**4 min**)
    - Rub the black skin off with your fingers or a paper towel.
    - Do not rinse it. Water washes away the smoky flavor.
    - Pull off the stem. Tear the chile open and scrape out the seeds.
    - Chop it into ½-inch pieces. Then wash your hands with soap. Chile on your fingers stings your eyes.
    - Goes into: The small bowl with the diced white onion
    - Look for: A few black flecks left on it are fine.
13. **7:56 AM — Take the bacon out** (**3 min**)
    - The bacon should be brown and crisp. If it is still soft, give it **2 more minutes**.
    - Burner off.
    - Lift the bacon out with the slotted spoon onto a plate lined with a paper towel.
    - The skillet handle is hot. Touch it only with an oven mitt or a folded dry towel.
    - Do not pour. Do not lift or tilt the hot skillet. Spoon the hot fat out of it into the mug.
    - Spoon 1 tablespoon of the fat back into the skillet.
    - Never pour fat down the drain. Let it harden in the mug, then put it in the trash.
    - You need:
        - Slotted spoon
        - Spoon
        - Plate with a paper towel
        - Heatproof mug
        - Oven mitt or dry towel
    - **Warning:** Hot fat burns. Keep it away from the flame.
14. **7:59 AM — Fry the onion and poblano** (**9 min**)
    - Burner on medium under the skillet with the bacon fat.
    - Add the diced white onion and the chopped poblano.
    - Start the timer. Stir every minute or so.
    - The tomatoes go in next. If the next card is something else, turn this burner off first.
    - **Timer: 8 minutes.** Onion and poblano. Stay here.
    - Look for: The onion is deep golden, brown at the edges.
    - Tip: This is a half batch in a big pan, so it can brown early. Brown at the edges before the timer ends? Stop and go on. Darkening fast? Turn the burner down to medium-low. Still pale when the timer ends? Give it **2 more minutes**.
15. **8:08 AM — Add the tomatoes and cook them dry** (**6 min**)
    - Add the drained tomatoes to the skillet.
    - Keep stirring until the watery liquid is gone, **4 to 6 minutes**.
    - Burner off. Leave it in the skillet.
    - Look for: Drag the spoon across the pan. No liquid runs back into the path.
16. **8:36 AM — Are the beans tender?** (**2 min**)
    - Fish out 3 beans with a spoon. Let them cool for **30 seconds**. They are very hot.
    - Press each one against the roof of your mouth with your tongue.
    - Tender means all 3 mash easily, like a soft potato. No hard or chalky center.
    - If even one is firm, tap Still firm. Beans soften much more slowly once the tomato mix goes in.
    - Not there yet is normal. Beans can need **90 minutes** or more, and longer if they did not soak overnight.
    - While you are there: stir, and add a splash of water if beans poke above the liquid.
    - Tender. Go on to the next step.
    - Still firm. Wait **15 minutes**. Then check again.
    - No time. Finish them at her place. Only from 10:10 AM on.
17. **8:38 AM — Add the good stuff to the beans** (**2 min**)
    - Scrape the onion, poblano and tomato mix into the bean pot. It is in the skillet, or in the spare tub if you packed it.
    - Add the bacon, if it is not already in with the mix, and ½ teaspoon of salt. Stir.
    - Keep it at a simmer with the lid slightly open.
    - **Timer: 25 minutes.** Beans: last simmer. Keep working. It ends about 9:05 AM.
    - At 8:52 AM: Stir the beans.
    - When it ends: Taste the beans. They are done.
18. **9:06 AM — Taste the beans. They are done.** (**3 min**)
    - Taste a spoonful of beans and broth. Flat? Add a pinch of salt, stir, and taste again. It can take 3 or 4 pinches.
    - It should be brothy, like a thick soup.
    - Too watery? Take the lid off. Turn the burner up to medium. Let it bubble until it looks like a thick soup. Stir often.
    - Burner off. Lid off so they cool for the fridge.
    - At her place with less than **1 hour** until dinner? Keep them hot instead. Lid on. Burner on its lowest setting.
    - **Timer: 25 minutes.** Beans cooling. Keep working. It ends about 9:34 AM.
    - When it ends: Beans into the fridge.
    - Tip: Optional, for a creamier broth: scoop 1 cup of the beans with a little liquid into a bowl. Mash them with a fork. Stir them back in.
19. **9:36 AM — Beans into the fridge** (**3 min**)
    - Scoop the beans and all their liquid into the big bean tub with a ladle or a clean mug. The pot is heavy and hot, so do not pour from it.
    - Lid on. Into the fridge, on a high shelf. Not next to the fish.
    - They taste even better tonight.
    - Goes into: Bean tub

### Her kitchen

20. **6:38 PM — Warm the beans** (**2 min**)
    - Take the bean tub out of the fridge. Tip the beans and all their liquid into the pot you set out for them. Scrape the tub out with a spoon. Already in a pot on the stove? Leave them there.
    - Lid on. Burner on medium-low.
    - Stir them each time you are at the stove, so the bottom does not stick.
    - Too thick? Add a splash of water.
    - You need:
        - Bean tub
        - Pot with a lid
        - Spoon
    - Not before 6:38 PM.
    - Look for: By dinner they are steaming hot, with bubbles all over.
21. **7:26 PM — Last touches** (**2 min**; shared step, it also covers other dishes)
    - The beans must be steaming and bubbling. Not yet? Turn the burner up to medium. Stir until they are.
    - Take the small bowl of chopped cilantro out of the fridge. Stir it into the beans.
    - Pull the 2 serranos out of the rice.
    - Check that every burner and the oven are off.
    - Beans, rice, corn and shrimp to the table. Never put a hot pot or pan on the bare table. Put a folded dry towel or a wooden board under it.
    - Not before 7:25 PM.

## Good to know

- An overnight soak saves about **an hour** in the morning. The **1-hour** quick soak works too.
- Beans are done when 3 of them mash easily against the roof of your mouth.
- They keep for **several days** in the fridge and taste better the next day.
- Freeze the leftover pork and bacon.
