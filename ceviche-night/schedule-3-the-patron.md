# The Patrón — Frijoles Charros (×0.5)

Source: Rick Bayless, *Authentic Mexican*.

- **Soak:** Mon night (or quick soak Tue 8:00 AM)
- **Start cooking:** Tue 8:00 AM (your kitchen)
- **Done:** about 10:30 AM
- **Reheat:** 5:45 PM (her kitchen)

## Equipment

- Heavy pot, 4 qt or larger, with a lid
- Skillet (bacon, onion, poblano)
- Tongs (roasting the poblano)
- Bowl + plate to cover (steaming the poblano)
- Slotted spoon
- Blender (only for the optional creamy-broth step)
- Container with a lid for transport

## Ingredients

- ½ lb dried pinto beans (about 1¼ cups)
- 1 quart water for soaking
- 3½ cups fresh water + ½ cup beer
- 2 oz fatty pork shoulder, ½-inch cubes
- 4 thick slices bacon, ½-inch pieces
- ½ medium white onion, diced
- 1 large poblano
- Half of a 15-oz can tomatoes, drained and chopped
- ½ tsp salt, or to taste
- ¼ cup cilantro, roughly chopped (goes in at dinner)

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Sort the beans: spread on a sheet pan, remove pebbles and shriveled beans. Rinse.
- [ ] Cube the pork
- [ ] Cut the bacon
- [ ] Dice the onion
- [ ] Drain and chop the tomatoes

## Steps

1. Soak. Cover the beans with 1 quart water. Skim off any that float. Soak **4–8 hours** (overnight is fine).
   - Quick soak: bring beans and water to a boil, boil **2 minutes**, turn off, cover, stand **1 hour**.
2. 8:00 AM — Drain. Add 3½ cups fresh water, ½ cup beer and the pork. Bring slowly to a boil. Partially cover. Simmer on medium-low **1–2 hours** until tender. Stir now and then. Add water if beans poke above the liquid.
3. 8:35 AM — Roast the poblano. Hold it with tongs directly over a gas flame, or put it under the broiler, turning until blackened all over. Put it in a bowl, cover with a plate, wait **5 minutes**. Rub off the skin, pull out the stem and seeds, chop.
4. 8:50 AM — Fry the bacon over medium-low about **10 minutes** until crisp. Lift it out. Keep 1 Tbsp of fat in the pan; pour off the rest.
5. 9:00 AM — Raise the heat to medium. Fry the onion and poblano in the fat about **8 minutes** until the onion is deep golden. Add the tomatoes and cook until their liquid is completely gone.
6. When the beans are tender: stir in the tomato mixture, bacon and salt. Simmer **20–30 minutes**.
7. Adjust. Too thin: simmer uncovered on higher heat. For a creamier broth: blend 1 cup of beans with their liquid and stir it back in.
8. Cool, then refrigerate. Transport cold.
9. 5:45 PM — Reheat slowly on low, stirring often. Add a splash of water if thick.
10. 6:26 PM — Stir in the cilantro.

## How you know it's done

- A bean mashes easily against the roof of your mouth.
- The broth is brothy but not watery.

## Storage and make-ahead

- Keeps several days in the fridge and improves overnight.
- Freeze the leftover pork shoulder and bacon.
