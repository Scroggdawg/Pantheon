The night before

Fish and shrimp into the fridge
  • Halibut: bottom shelf, at the back. That is the coldest spot.
  • Keep it in its wrapper, on a plate.
  • Shrimp: same shelf.
Soak the beans tonight (3 minutes)
  • Pour half the bag of pinto beans (about 1¼ cups) onto a plate.
  • Pick out any pebbles or shriveled beans.
  • Rinse the beans in a strainer.
  • Put them in a big bowl with 4 cups of cold water. Leave it on the counter.
  • Do this one. With the soak, the beans are done with more than an hour to spare.
  • Without it there is a 1-hour shortcut. It uses up almost all the spare time in your morning.
  • The beans may then have to finish cooking at her place.
Ice packs into the freezer
  • You need them frozen for the cooler tomorrow afternoon.
  • No ice packs? Buy a bag of ice on your way home tomorrow.
Find your cooler and a box for the gear
  • The cooler has to hold the big bean tub, about 10 jars, the fish, the shrimp and the ice.
  • No cooler? A sturdy box lined with a trash bag works. Buy 2 bags of ice tomorrow.
  • The gear is about 20 things. Find a box, a laundry basket or two strong bags for it.
Text her 3 questions
  • Does your oven broiler work?
  • Do you have a microwave?
  • Can you clear a fridge shelf? Two big bowls have to fit.
  • Ask what she has from the gear list. Leave those things at home.
Find 10 jars or tubs, 1 big tub, 3 zip bags
  • All with lids that seal. They ride in a cooler.
  • 1 quart jar (4 cups) for the pickled onions.
  • 5 jars or tubs that hold about 2 cups: slaw dressing, rice broth, rice purée, corn sauce, and one spare.
  • 4 small jars or tubs, about 1 cup each: two for lime juice, one for the marinade, one for the pink sauce.
  • 1 big tub, 2 quarts (8 cups), for the beans.
  • 3 zip bags: one for the rice, two for the fish and the shrimp in the cooler.
  • Short on jars? A mug covered tightly with foil works, packed upright.
Check the pantry
  • You said you have these. Look now.
  • Salt, black pepper, whole peppercorns, sugar, bay leaves.
  • Ground cumin, chipotle powder, dried Mexican oregano.
  • White vinegar, vegetable oil, olive oil, sriracha, chicken broth, white rice.
  • Also find: a can opener and an oven mitt.
  • Something missing? Buy it tonight if you can.
Check the avocados
  • Squeeze one gently.
  • If it gives a little, put both in the fridge.
  • Rock hard? Leave them on the counter in a paper bag.
Charge your phone. Set Auto-Lock to Never.
  • The timers cannot ring while the phone is locked.
  • Keep the screen on: Settings, Display & Brightness, Auto-Lock, Never.
  • Low Power Mode off. It forces the screen to lock after 30 seconds.

12. Blacken the poblano

8 min
  • You are burning the skin on purpose.
  • Gas stove: turn a burner to high. Lay the chile on the metal grate, right in the flame.
  • Turn it with tongs every 30 seconds until the skin is black and blistered all over, 5 to 8 minutes.
  • No gas flame: move an oven rack to about 4 inches below the top element. Turn the broiler to high.
  • Put the chile on a foil-lined pan on that rack. Turn it with tongs every 2 to 3 minutes, about 10 minutes. Use an oven mitt.
  • Drop the hot chile in a bowl and cover the bowl with a plate. Burner or broiler off.

You need: Poblano (labeled pasilla) · Tongs · Bowl and plate · No gas flame: foil-lined pan and oven mitt

Timer: 5 minutes. Poblano steaming. Keep working. It ends about 7:46 AM.
When it ends: Peel and chop the poblano.

Look for: Black on every side. No big green patches.

Tip: The skin may pop or catch small flames. That is normal. Keep turning.

WARNING: Open flame. Keep sleeves, towels and paper away from it. Turn on the fan over the stove.

22. Zest half a lime, then juice eleven

18 min

Glossary

Bacon fat

The melted fat left in the pan after frying bacon.

It is the cooking oil for the next step.

Blanch

Dunk food in very hot water for a short time, then drain it.

Here it takes the harsh bite off the raw onion and helps it turn pink.

Broiler

The heating element at the top of the oven. It cooks from above with very high heat.

Set the oven to Broil, High.

The food goes on a rack near the top.

In some gas ovens the broiler is a drawer under the oven instead.

Char

Burn the skin on purpose until it is black and blistered.

The skin gets peeled off. The flesh underneath turns soft and smoky.

Cilantro or parsley?

You have 2 bunches of cilantro and 1 bunch of parsley. They look alike.

Cilantro leaves are rounded and soft. Rub one. It smells sharp and strong.

Parsley leaves are pointed and darker. It smells mild.

The band or twist tie often names the herb.

Coldest spot

The bottom shelf of the fridge, at the back.

Cook until dry

Keep cooking and stirring until the watery liquid is gone.

Test: drag the spoon across the pan. If no liquid runs back into the path, it is done.

Crumble

Break it into small bits with your fingers.

Cure

Lime juice firms up raw fish and turns it white, the way cooking does.

It does not kill germs or parasites. That is why the fish must be very fresh and stay cold.

Cutting safely

Put a damp paper towel under the cutting board so it cannot slide.

Cut anything round in half first. Lay the flat side down so it cannot roll.

Hold the food with your fingertips curled under. Your knuckles face the blade.

Cut slowly. Never try to catch a falling knife.

Damp towel

Wet a clean kitchen towel and wring it out hard.

It should feel damp, not dripping.

Deep golden

Past soft and see-through.

The onion is brown at the edges, the color of honey.

Dice

Cut into small cubes.

Slice into strips, then cut across the strips.

¼ inch is about the size of a pea.

Float freely

There is enough juice that the pieces move around when you stir.

If the fish is packed tight, the juice cannot reach every side.

Fluff

Drag a fork through the rice to separate the grains.

Do not stir or press. That makes it mushy.

Fold

Mix gently so nothing gets mashed.

Slide a spoon down the side of the bowl, under, and up over the top.

Turn the bowl and repeat.

Full boil

Big bubbles all over the surface that do not stop when you stir.

Garlic clove

A whole garlic is called a head. Do not use the whole head.

Break off one segment. That is a clove.

Press down on it with the bottom of a mug until it cracks.

Peel off the papery skin. Cut off the hard end.

Half-moons

Cut the onion in half from top to bottom.

Lay each half flat side down.

Slice across it. Each slice falls apart into half-circles.

Heat levels

On a dial marked 1 to 10:

Low is 2.

Medium-low is 3 to 4.

Medium is 5.

Medium-high is 7.

High is 9 to 10.

Other numbers on the dial? Medium is the middle number.

Only Lo, Med and Hi? Medium-low is halfway between Lo and Med. Medium-high is halfway between Med and Hi.

Gas with no marks? Look at the flame. Low is the smallest steady flame. High is the biggest flame that stays under the pan. Medium is halfway between.

If the food bubbles or browns too fast, turn the burner down.

Husk corn

Peel the green leaves down and snap them off at the bottom.

Pull off the silky threads.

Lid slightly open

Put the lid on, then slide it a little to one side.

Leave a gap about as wide as a finger so steam can escape.

Loose C

A cooked shrimp curls into the letter C.

If it curls tight into an O, it is overcooked.

Measuring spoons

Use the set of measuring spoons, not the spoons you eat with.

Each one is stamped with its size.

TBSP or 15 mL is the tablespoon. It is the biggest.

TSP or 5 mL is the teaspoon.

The smaller ones say ½ and ¼.

Fill the spoon. Scrape it level with the back of a knife.

1½ teaspoons means one 1 teaspoon plus one ½ teaspoon.

3 teaspoons make 1 tablespoon. 4 tablespoons make ¼ cup.

Milky white

Raw rice grains look a little see-through.

After a few minutes in hot oil they turn solid, chalky white.

Mince

Chop as small as you can.

Rock the knife back and forth over the pile until the pieces are tiny.

Opaque

Solid white all the way through.

No shiny, see-through center.

Open an avocado

Lay the avocado on the cutting board. Do not hold it in your hand.

Cut into it the long way until the knife hits the pit.

Roll the avocado so the cut goes all the way around the pit.

Twist the two halves apart.

Need the half with the pit? Scoop the pit out with a spoon.

Pat dry

Press paper towels onto the food to soak up the water on its surface.

Dry food browns. Wet food steams.

Pinch

What you can pick up between your thumb and two fingers.

Purée

Blend until completely smooth, with no chunks.

Roughly chop

A few quick cuts.

Uneven pieces about the size of your fingernail are fine.

Scallions

Also called green onions.

Cut off the hairy root end.

Use both the white and the green parts.

Sear

Cook fast in a very hot pan so the outside browns.

Lay the food down. Do not move it until it is time to turn it.

Seed a chile

Cut off the stem. Slice the chile in half the long way.

Scrape out the seeds and the pale ribs with the tip of a spoon.

That is where most of the heat is.

Wash your hands with soap after. Do not touch your eyes.

Shimmering oil

Hot oil looks thin and shiny. Faint ripples move across it.

You may see the first thin wisp of smoke.

That is the moment to add the food.

Do not tilt the pan to check. The handle is very hot.

Shred cabbage

Cut the cabbage into wedges through the core.

Cut the hard core off a wedge.

Lay the wedge flat and slice across it as thin as you can.

The slices fall into ribbons.

Simmer

Just below boiling.

A few small bubbles break the surface every second or two.

If it is bubbling hard, turn the heat down.

Slit

One shallow cut down the side of the chile, from stem to tip.

The chile stays whole but lets its flavor out.

Strainer

A bowl full of holes, also called a colander.

Set it in the sink. Liquid runs through. The food stays.

Tent with foil

Lay a sheet of foil loosely over the top.

It holds the heat in.

Toast a spice

Heat a dry pan with no oil.

Add the spice and keep it moving until you can smell it, about 30 seconds.

Tip it out right away. It burns fast.

Wedges

Cut the lime in half from tip to tip.

Cut each half the long way into 3 pieces.

Zest

The thin green outer skin of the lime. It holds the lime smell.

Scrape it off with a fine grater. Or peel thin strips with a vegetable peeler and chop them tiny.

Stop when you reach the white layer. The white part is bitter.
